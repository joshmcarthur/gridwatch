# Site copy

Rules for published Gridwatch prose. This is for pages, not for chat.

## Reader

Someone curious about how New Zealand's electricity market works. They might have a bill in mind, or a news story. They might not. Curiosity is enough. Don't write as if the only way in is "after a bill, or after a headline."

## Voice

The speaker is Gridwatch, or this site. No I. No we, except in a page title that already says it (How we do this). You is fine when the reader is in the sentence: your bill, you pay.

Start with the public files and why they are hard going. Then say what this site does. Don't introduce Gridwatch like an institution. Don't write a hero origin. Don't call this an observatory.

Write the way you'd explain it to someone curious, not like a press release. A blunt line is fine ("they're a slog"). Slogans are not.

## Jargon

Unexplained acronyms are banned on every page. First use of EMI is Electricity Market Information, the Authority's public files. Same for wholesale, spot, system operator, Transpower, bronze, silver, gold.

Use the same word for the same thing. Files are files. Pages are pages. The household bill is a bill. The company on it is a retailer. The wholesale number is a wholesale price.

On About, How, and Terms, never use a term or acronym without explaining it on that page, in the same breath or the next sentence.

Daily pages follow Daily pages below. They may use a short, consistent label and a link instead of repeating the lecture.

## Daily pages

A daily page explains that day. It does not re-teach the market.

When the day has an unusual stretch, that line opens the page. A quiet day uses the figure furthest from usual, or one sentence about the kind of day, then a few labelled numbers. It must not define megawatt-hours, wholesale vs a household bill, or the Cook Strait cable. Those belong on About, and in a compact "Words on this page" glossary on the day so a cold link still has an escape hatch.

Link the short labels (wholesale price, megawatt-hour, the two islands) to that glossary, and from the glossary on to About. Same word for the same thing.

Method caveats about **this day's numbers** stay with the chart: incomplete files, residual, proxy prices, batteries as storage. Put the long method in a "How to read this" disclosure. The visible caption is one line.

Clock times in public sentences, not trading period numbers. Place names in body copy (Ōtāhuhu, Haywards, Benmore). Point-of-connection codes belong in the numbers table and in hover text.

Completeness is a warning when a day is incomplete. A complete day does not lead with a pipeline status.

## About, How, Terms

About walks a curious person through the market: wholesale vs the bill, prices in short slices of the day, megawatt-hours, who publishes EMI, who runs the grid, the two islands, hydro and wind, and that this site looks at days that have already finished.

How we do this is method: sources, processing, published data. Not the market primer.

Terms is limits: what the numbers are for, and what they are not for. Not About, not How.

Independence (not the Authority, not Transpower) belongs where those two are named. Don't lead with a disclaimer.

## Files

The EMI files are complex. They were built for market systems, not necessarily for people. Don't call them hostile, ugly, or a mess. Don't apologise for them either.

## Headlines and drama

Don't write as if headlines are hiding something. If the North Island and the South Island can have different prices, say that. Stop there.

Don't puff significance. Don't turn "the files are hard" into a brand story about a gap in the world.

## Diagrams

Diagrams are allowed, and they should carry some of the explanation when they can. Labels still need the jargon rule for that page. The page copy must still make sense if the diagram is missing.

- How we do this: sources → processing → published data, and bronze / silver / gold treatment on the paragraphs that explain those layers (raw files, cleaned tables, published site data, or whatever the repo actually means).
- About: a small sketch of the market is fine (stations → wholesale → retailer → bill; or Authority / Transpower / this site).
- Terms: a small use / don't-use split is fine. Don't put the pipeline there.

## Prefer

this site, Gridwatch, files, pages, retailer, wholesale price, bill, Electricity Authority, EMI (after spelling it out), Transpower, system operator, slice of the day, the industry, Ōtāhuhu, Haywards, Benmore, megawatt-hour, three places, electricity generated

## Don't

I, we (in body copy), observatory, evidence-led, hostile, "the gap", "exists because of that", "headlines hide", "rewrite your bill", pretty, serves as, it's not X it's Y, unexplained acronyms, "work there" when you mean the industry, trading-period numbers in public sentences, POC codes in daily body copy, "dataset", "grouped readings", "sample points", "Power made" (it is energy, not power), "trading day" on the home and daily pages (How we do this explains it), claims about the two islands drawn from the three places

Run published copy through the humanize skill before it ships.
