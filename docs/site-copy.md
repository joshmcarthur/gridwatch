# Site copy

Rules for published Gridwatch prose. This is for pages, not for chat.

## Reader

Someone curious about how New Zealand's electricity market works. They might have a bill in mind, or a news story. They might not. Curiosity is enough. Don't write as if the only way in is "after a bill, or after a headline."

## Voice

The speaker is Gridwatch, or this site. No I. No we, except in a page title that already says it (How we do this). You is fine when the reader is in the sentence: your bill, you pay.

Start with the public files and why they are hard going. Then say what this site does. Don't introduce Gridwatch like an institution. Don't write a hero origin. Don't call this an observatory.

Write the way you'd explain it to someone curious, not like a press release. A blunt line is fine ("they're a slog"). Slogans are not.

## Jargon

Never use a term or acronym without explaining it on that page, in the same breath or the next sentence. First use of EMI is Electricity Market Information, the Authority's public files. Same for wholesale, spot, system operator, Transpower, bronze, silver, gold.

Use the same word for the same thing. Files are files. Pages are pages. The household bill is a bill. The company on it is a retailer. The wholesale number is a wholesale price.

## About, How, Terms

About walks a curious person through the market: wholesale vs the bill, prices in short slices of the day, who publishes EMI, who runs the grid, the two islands, hydro and wind, and that this site looks at days that have already finished.

How we do this is method: sources, processing, published data. Not the market primer.

Terms is limits: what the numbers are for, and what they are not for. Not About, not How.

Independence (not the Authority, not Transpower) belongs where those two are named. Don't lead with a disclaimer.

## Files

The EMI files are complex. They were built for market systems, not necessarily for people. Don't call them hostile, ugly, or a mess. Don't apologise for them either.

## Headlines and drama

Don't write as if headlines are hiding something. If the North Island and the South Island can have different prices, say that. Stop there.

Don't puff significance. Don't turn "the files are hard" into a brand story about a gap in the world.

## Diagrams

Diagrams are allowed, and they should carry some of the explanation when they can. Labels still need the jargon rule. The page copy must still make sense if the diagram is missing.

- How we do this: sources → processing → published data, and bronze / silver / gold treatment on the paragraphs that explain those layers (raw files, cleaned tables, published site data, or whatever the repo actually means).
- About: a small sketch of the market is fine (stations → wholesale → retailer → bill; or Authority / Transpower / this site).
- Terms: a small use / don't-use split is fine. Don't put the pipeline there.

## Prefer

this site, Gridwatch, files, pages, retailer, wholesale price, bill, Electricity Authority, EMI (after spelling it out), Transpower, system operator, slice of the day, the industry

## Don't

I, we (in body copy), observatory, evidence-led, hostile, "the gap", "exists because of that", "headlines hide", "rewrite your bill", pretty, serves as, it's not X it's Y, unexplained acronyms, "work there" when you mean the industry

Run published copy through the humanize skill before it ships.
