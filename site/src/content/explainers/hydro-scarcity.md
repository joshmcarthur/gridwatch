title: Hydro scarcity
description: What it means when the lakes run low, and why that is a different story from one expensive evening.

## Simple explanation

New Zealand gets a lot of its electricity from hydro lakes. Rain and snow fill the lakes. Stations then run water through turbines. When inflows stay low for months, the lakes run down. Other stations have to make up the difference, and wholesale prices tend to rise.

That long shortage of water is hydro scarcity.

## A more technical explanation

Hydro is stored energy. Operators choose when to run water today against keeping it for later. When storage is high, hydro can cover a lot of the load and keep more expensive plants off. When storage is low, those more expensive plants run more, and the wholesale price tends to sit higher for weeks.

A spike can still happen when the lakes are full: a still evening, a full Cook Strait link, or tight reserve can lift the price while there is plenty of water in the catchments.

How much hydro generated on a day is not a lake level, an inflow, or a water-value. A large hydro share does not mean the lakes were scarce, or that they were full.
