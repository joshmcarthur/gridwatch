title: Frequency keeping
description: The service that holds the grid at 50 Hz from moment to moment, and how it differs from reserve held for a trip.

## Simple explanation

The New Zealand grid runs at 50 hertz (50 Hz). Hertz is cycles per second: the rate the alternating current swings. If generation and load drift apart, that rate moves. Frequency keeping is the service that trims output from moment to moment so the rate stays near 50 Hz.

It is not the same as instantaneous reserve. Reserve is spare capacity held ready in case a plant trips. Frequency keeping is already working in ordinary seconds, before anyone trips.

## A more technical explanation

A small mismatch between generation and load shows up as a small change in frequency. Frequency keeping plants raise or lower output continuously to correct that. Instantaneous reserve sits unused until a large trip, then it has to act in 6 seconds or 60 seconds. [Instantaneous reserves](/explainers/instantaneous-reserves/) covers that product.

The two have different prices, when they have prices at all. This site publishes the wholesale prices for fast reserve and slow reserve. It does not publish a frequency-keeping price, and it does not chart system frequency. A reserve spike on a daily page is not a stand-in for frequency keeping.

The Electricity Authority is the public body that sets the market rules. Transpower, as system operator, keeps the high-voltage network working from minute to minute, including frequency. This site reads the public files after the day is over. It is separate from both.
