title: Frequency keeping
description: The service that holds the grid at 50 Hz from moment to moment, and how it differs from reserve held for a trip.

## Simple explanation

The New Zealand grid runs at 50 hertz (50 Hz). Hertz is cycles per second: the rate the alternating current swings. If generation and load drift apart, that rate moves. Frequency keeping is the service that trims output from moment to moment so the rate stays near 50 Hz.

It is not the same as instantaneous reserve. Reserve is spare capacity held ready in case a plant trips. Frequency keeping is always working.

## A more technical explanation

A small mismatch between generation and load shows up as a small change in frequency. Frequency keeping plants raise or lower output continuously to correct that. Instantaneous reserve sits unused until a large trip. [Instantaneous reserves](/explainers/instantaneous-reserves/) describes how this works.
