---
title: Wholesale prices and the bill
description: Why the wholesale number and the household bill are different prices, even though they are both for electricity.
---

## Simple explanation

Power stations do not bill households. They offer electricity in bulk into a wholesale market, and retailers buy it there. A retailer is the company named on a household bill.

The wholesale price is what those two sides pay each other in a short slice of the day. The bill is a retail plan. Most household plans do not pass every half-hour spike through. A few plans do. Either way, the number on the bill is the plan, not the wholesale price from last Tuesday evening.

## A more technical explanation

Wholesale prices are set in half-hour slices, at each named place on the grid. They can move a long way from one slice to the next. A megawatt-hour is 1,000 kilowatt-hours, the same kWh unit as a line on a bill, so the units can be converted. Conversion is not a household rate. A plan may be a fixed cents-per-kWh, a bundled daily charge, or a plan that tracks the wholesale price. This site has none of those retail rates.

Gentailers both generate and retail. That does not make the wholesale price the bill. The wholesale number is still the market price. The bill is still the contract with the retailer.

[Nodal pricing](/explainers/nodal-pricing/) covers why the wholesale price can differ by place. About walks through the same split, from stations to the bill.

This site looks at days that have already finished. It does not say what a household paid, and it does not rewrite a bill.
