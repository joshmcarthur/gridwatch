## Simple explanation

A cable under Cook Strait carries electricity between the North Island and the South Island. That cable is a high-voltage direct current (HVDC) link, and it can only carry so much energy.

When the link can carry enough, cheaper electricity on one island can serve the other. When it cannot, the two islands behave more like separate markets, and wholesale prices pull apart.

## A more technical explanation

The South Island end of the link is at Benmore in the Waitaki, and the North Island end is at Haywards near Wellington. Offtake is energy taken from the alternating-current grid into the link. Injection is energy put from the link back into the grid.

Some of the energy that enters the cable at one end turns into heat on the way, so less arrives at the other end. That shortfall is the link loss.

Wholesale prices at Haywards and Benmore, and the spread between them, are on [nodal pricing](/explainers/nodal-pricing/).

## Historical example

On Monday 27 July 2026 the link ran north all day. Haywards received 21,493.034 megawatt-hours, 895.5 megawatts as a day average. The highest half-hour average was 1,098.0 megawatts, from 4:30pm to 5:00pm.

By the evening price spike it had dropped to 966.4 megawatts, in the half-hour from 5:30pm to 6:00pm.
